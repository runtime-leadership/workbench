# Public Engineering Career Ladder Library

> Part of [Workbench by Runtime Leadership](https://github.com/runtime-leadership/workbench). Use this library on its own or in a private Workbench created from the GitHub template.

Public career ladders can help engineers and managers compare how different organizations describe roles, levels, and growth. Use them as references for better conversations, not as universal definitions or promotion checklists.

## Career Frameworks

### Engineering Ladders

[Open Engineering Ladders](https://github.com/jorgef/engineeringladders)

**Tracks:** Developer, Tech Lead, Technical Program Manager, and Engineering Manager.

**What is different:** Radar charts compare Technology, System, People, Process, and Influence. Tech Lead is a distinct track rather than a generic senior-engineer title.

**License:** Apache 2.0.

### Dropbox Engineering Career Framework

[Open the Dropbox Engineering Career Framework](https://dropbox.github.io/dbx-career-framework/)

**Tracks:** Software engineering IC1 through IC7 and engineering management M3 through M7, plus reliability, machine learning, data, security, quality, SDET, and TPM roles.

**What is different:** Dropbox separates level expectations from role-specific craft responsibilities and includes promotion principles and senior-engineer archetypes.

**License:** Apache 2.0.

### Financial Times Engineering Progression

[Open Financial Times Engineering Progression](https://engineering-progression.ft.com/competencies/) or [inspect its source on GitHub](https://github.com/Financial-Times/engineering-progression).

**Tracks:** Junior Engineer through Technology Director, with individual contributor and people-manager branches at senior and principal levels.

**Why it is useful:** The current framework is public, version-controlled, and maintained as the source of truth for roles in the Financial Times technology organization. It separates current and legacy competencies, supports both IC and people-manager paths, and makes the underlying job-family data available for inspection.

**License:** MIT.

### Monzo Engineering Progression Frameworks

[Open the engineering framework](https://monzo.com/documents/engineering-progression-framework-v2-0.pdf), [open the engineering-manager framework](https://monzo.com/documents/engineering-manager-framework-v1-0.pdf), or [read Monzo's implementation lessons](https://monzo.com/blog/lessons-from-implementing-progression-frameworks-at-scale).

**Tracks:** Engineering individual contributor Levels 1 through 6. The management framework covers Engineering Manager, Senior Engineering Manager, Engineering Director, Vice President of Engineering, and CTO.

**Why it is useful:** Monzo published both IC and engineering-management frameworks, then documented why its earlier framework became a checkbox exercise. The revised model centers impact, technical skills, and behaviors for engineers and Team and Execution, People, and Leadership for managers.

**Published:** 2021. Use these as historical implementation references rather than assuming they remain Monzo's current internal process.

### Devexperts Engineering Grading System

[Open the Devexperts Engineering Grading System](https://github.com/devexperts/grading-system).

**Tracks:** Engineer E1 through E7 and Engineering Manager EM1 through EM3.

**What is different:** Separate IC and manager usage guides connect level expectations to recurring 1:1s. The framework includes behaviors to pursue and avoid alongside technical and role expectations.

**License:** CC BY 4.0.

### Sarah Drasner's Career Ladders

[Open the engineering career ladder](https://career-ladders.dev/engineering/).

**Tracks:** Engineer I through Distinguished Engineer, plus Developer Experience and Documentation ladders.

**What is different:** Tech Lead is defined as a project role that someone can hold at any career stage, not as a promotion level. The expectations use direct, practical language.

**License:** MIT.

## How to Use a Career Ladder

Start with your employer's framework when one exists. Use an external ladder when it clarifies an ambiguous expectation, supplies a missing dimension, or gives both people better language for a growth conversation.

1. Compare current-level and next-level expectations.
2. Ask both people to bring evidence for their views.
3. Discuss where their interpretations agree and differ.
4. Separate a capability gap from a lack of opportunity to demonstrate the capability.
5. Choose one to three development priorities rather than working through every line.
6. Connect those priorities to real work, feedback, and manager support.
7. Revisit the goals when the work or priorities change.

Use the [Engineering Level Growth Conversation Template](../templates/engineering-level-growth-conversation/template.md) to turn that discussion into focused objectives and follow-through.

If the gap you keep running into is specifically about leading projects rather than overall engineering competency, for example planning, de-risking, operational readiness, stakeholder communication, or technical influence, the [Project Leadership Capability Radar](project-leadership-radar.md) gives that narrower slice its own dedicated language and a visual growth curve across levels.

## Keep the Limits Clear

- Titles do not map consistently between companies.
- An external ladder does not override your employer's expectations or promotion process.
- A ladder should support judgment and dialogue, not calculate someone's level.
- Completing every item does not guarantee promotion.
- Managers are responsible for creating opportunities to practice and demonstrate the capabilities they expect.
- Check the source license before copying or adapting substantial content.
