# Engineering 1:1 Preparation Guide

> Part of [Workbench by Runtime Leadership](https://github.com/runtime-leadership/workbench). Use this resource on its own or in a private Workbench created from the GitHub template.

Use this guide before a 1:1 to choose one or two useful topics. It is a preparation menu, not an agenda to complete and not a script for the conversation.

## Before the Conversation

- Review the previous entry in the shared 1:1 document.
- Check whether either person made a commitment that needs follow-through.
- Notice topics that keep returning without a decision or change.
- Add only the questions or topics that would make this conversation more useful.

## Conversation Menu

### Support and Context

- Where would support, context, or feedback help?
- What is making the work easier or harder?
- Is there a decision or unresolved issue that deserves time?

### Feedback and Recognition

- Is there feedback that should not wait?
- Is there work or progress worth recognizing?
- What might one person see that the other does not?

### Motivation, Workload, and Growth

- Is motivation, energy, workload, or confidence changing?
- Is there work the engineer wants more or less of?
- Is an opportunity, expectation, or growth question becoming important?

### Follow-Through and Patterns

- What commitment from the previous conversation needs attention?
- Which topic keeps coming up without being resolved?
- Is there anything ordinary project meetings are not creating space to discuss?

For a dedicated level and development discussion, use the [Engineering Level Growth Conversation Template](../templates/engineering-level-growth-conversation/template.md).

## Take Brief Notes

Stay engaged in the conversation. You are not a stenographer, and the shared document should not become a transcript.

Capture only what will be useful later:

- context needed to understand a key point
- a decision or changed understanding
- an action and owner
- something worth revisiting

If writing during the conversation is distracting, take a few minutes immediately afterward to add the useful points.

## Use the History

The running 1:1 history can help both people see recurring topics, missed follow-through, changing concerns, and progress that would be difficult to reconstruct from memory.

Use that history to support the relationship and improve future conversations. Do not turn it into covert performance surveillance or a private dossier. Follow company policy and keep sensitive information in approved systems.
